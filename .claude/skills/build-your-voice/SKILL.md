# Build Your Voice

Work out how this owner really writes and talks from REAL samples, then write brand/voice.md with rules that can be followed. The test for every rule: does it change how a sentence gets written? "Friendly and authentic" fails that test. "Opens with the point, one comma max, swears occasionally" passes.

This is the first pass at the voice file and it is supposed to be. A later session feeds a whole library of their long-form material through NotebookLM and deepens the same file. Say that out loud so the owner is not trying to make this perfect today.

## Collect the raw material first

1. Ask for three to five things they actually wrote where they sound like themselves. Push them toward the scattered stuff: emails to clients, the reply where they explained their thing to somebody who was not getting it, a text to a customer, a long comment they left somewhere. That material beats anything polished, because they were not performing for anybody when they wrote it, they were just trying to be understood.

2. Then one fresh sample: have them talk out loud for two minutes about something in their business they care about, dictated, not typed. Raw speech is the truest sample there is. Typed answers get cleaned up without them noticing.

3. If they have published content, take one piece, but weight it least. Published work is usually them performing.

4. Then the negative space. Ask for two or three pieces of marketing from their industry that make them cringe. What they would never sound like defines the banned list far better than guessing does.

If they have long-form material (videos, podcasts, newsletters, recorded calls), do not try to process it here. Tell them it gets handled properly in the notebook pass and move on.

## Derive the rules

Study the samples for patterns, then write rules that are concrete enough to follow. Look for how they open, how they close, their sentence length and rhythm, the words and phrases they genuinely repeat, their punctuation habits, the casual grammar they use on purpose, and their quirks: fragments, asides, lowercase, self-corrections.

Kill any rule that is an adjective in disguise. If it does not change the next sentence someone writes, it is not a rule.

Also set the dial: where the voice runs looser and where it runs tighter. Text-to-a-friend for a post, one notch straighter for a sales page. Same voice, two settings.

## Prove it before filing

Write each of three test sentences TWICE, once in the derived voice and once competent but generic, unlabeled: a post opener, a reply to a customer, one line of page copy. Let them pick which one is theirs. If they cannot tell, the rules are not sharp yet. Adjust from their reactions, never from your taste.

## Write it into brand/voice.md

Use these headings, and keep anything already in the file that survived. Every voice session in this department writes into this same shape, so later passes deepen it instead of fighting it:

- The Sound: one line, how they actually write and talk, in plain terms and not adjectives.
- How I Open.
- How I Close.
- Rhythm: sentence length and pacing.
- Phrases I Use: the words and expressions that are genuinely theirs.
- Words I Never Use: derived from the cringe samples, not invented.
- Quirks: fragments, asides, lowercase, punctuation habits, whatever repeats.
- The Dial: looser and tighter settings.
- Never Sound Like This: lines that felt fake to them, added to over time.
- Self-Check: read it aloud, a stumble means rewrite.

Mark anything thin as provisional so the next pass knows where to dig. Note at the bottom that every future correction about sounding wrong updates THIS file, not just the draft that missed.

## The ending, always

1. Read the one-line sound back to them.
2. If the samples were too thin to derive real rules, write the rules you could, mark the rest provisional, and add more voice samples to business.md's Still to answer list.
3. Tell them in one line that from here on every writing hire reads this file first, which is why corrections compound instead of repeating.
4. Save the work to GitHub by running the github agent's save-work skill at departments/it-systems/dev-team/github-agent/skills/save-work/.

## Done when

brand/voice.md holds the headings above filled with rules a stranger could follow and produce something that sounds like the owner, the test sentences passed their judgment, thin areas are marked provisional rather than guessed, and the work is saved.
