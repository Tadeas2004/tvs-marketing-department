# Build Your Offer

An ICP says who. A contrarian take says what you stand for. An offer is the thing a person hands you money for. Without it, everything the department writes points nowhere.

## Before asking anything

Read business.md, the audit report, brand/icp.md, brand/contrarian-take.md, and brand/friction-points.md. Read brand/offer.md so you know what is already there.

The ICP file holds their one-liner: I help [person] go from [zero state] to [hero state] so they can achieve [outcome]. That sentence is the promise. This session builds the vehicle that delivers it.

## Pick the fork, do not ask them to choose

From their business file, decide which of these they are, say which one and why in one sentence, and let them confirm or redirect:

- They already sell something. The job is naming it precisely and sharpening it. Most owners describe what they sell in words nobody outside their business understands.
- They sell nothing yet. The job is building the first one from their ICP and friction points. It is a starting offer to test on a real buyer, not a forever decision, and say that plainly so they stop trying to get it perfect.

## The four dials

Every offer runs on four dials. Walk them one at a time, in the owner's own words, and write what they say:

1. How big is the outcome? What actually changes for this person, in their life or their numbers.
2. How believable is it that THEY will get it? Not that it works in general, that it works for someone like them. Proof, track record, a guarantee, or a process they can see.
3. How long does it take? The honest timeline to the first real result, not the full transformation.
4. How much work is it on their end? What they have to do, learn, or give up.

The first two go up, the last two come down. Any change that raises one and does not hurt the others is worth making. Ask the owner what they could change on each dial today, and push when the answer is "nothing."

## Answer the five

Open their friction points and walk all five against the offer, one at a time. For each one, ask which is true:

- The offer already answers it. Say how, in a sentence, and note it.
- The offer could answer it if you built the answer in. This is the move. Scared it will not work for them becomes a guarantee. Worried it takes too long becomes a timeline promise. No time becomes done-for-you instead of taught. Change the offer right here.
- Nothing in the offer fixes it, so content and conversations have to handle it. Mark it that way so the writer knows it is their job.

An offer that answers three or four of their five friction points is a strong offer. If it answers none, the offer is aimed at a problem the buyer does not have, and that is worth saying out loud.

## Write it plain

Get concrete answers to all of these, and push on every vague one:

- What is it called, in words a stranger understands.
- What do they actually get. Deliverables, sessions, access, the thing in their hands.
- How it works, start to finish, in three or four steps.
- How long it takes.
- What it costs. A number or a starting number, never "it depends" as a final answer.
- What happens if it does not work. A guarantee, a make-it-right rule, or an honest "no refunds and here is why."

Pushback rules: consulting, coaching, and marketing services are categories, not offers, push until a stranger could picture what arrives. A price of "depends on the project" gets pushed to a starting number. If the outcome could describe any competitor, push. And check the math with them once: if the price does not leave them real money after the work, it is not an offer, it is a favor.

## Write it into the repo

Update brand/offer.md with these sections: The Offer (one plain sentence a stranger would understand). The Promise (their one-liner from the ICP file). What They Get. How It Works. Timeline. What It Costs. What Happens If It Does Not Work. Friction It Answers (which of their five, and how). Left For Content (the friction points the offer does not fix, flagged for the writer).

Read it back in under a minute and let the owner correct before saving. Their words, cleaned but never corporatized.

## The ending, always

1. Say the offer back in one sentence and ask whether they would say it out loud to a stranger without flinching. If not, it is not plain enough yet.
2. Tell them in one line that this file is what every content and page skill reads from here on, and that it gets sharpened the first time a real buyer says no.
3. Save the work to GitHub by running the github agent's save-work skill at departments/it-systems/dev-team/github-agent/skills/save-work/.

## Done when

brand/offer.md names what someone buys in plain words, carries a real price and a real timeline, states what happens if it does not work, shows which friction points it answers and which are left for content, and the work is saved.
