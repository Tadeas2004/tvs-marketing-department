# Find Your Contrarian Take

Most owners think they are supposed to invent something edgy. They are not. The take is already in them as the thing they do differently and believe quietly. This surfaces several of them, picks the one they lead with today, and files the rest as candidates, because the audience decides which one wins, not the owner at their desk.

## Before asking anything

Read business.md, the audit report, and brand/icp.md. Read brand/contrarian-take.md so you know what is already there. If the brain dump filed a raw quote about what most people in their industry get wrong, open by reading it back: you said this, say more. If a heading in that file still says Raw Material From Day 1, rename it Raw Material From the Brain Dump while you are in there.

## The two column sweep

This is the core of the session and you run it right here in the chat, building the table as they talk. Two columns:

- LEFT: what gets said or done in their industry that they disagree with or would do differently.
- RIGHT: what they actually believe and do instead.

Tell them plainly what you are doing, then fill the left column by asking, one question at a time, letting them ramble on each:

- What common advice in your space makes you cringe? What have you watched it do to people who followed it?
- What do customers believe when they show up that you have to talk them out of? That belief came from your competitors' marketing.
- What do you do differently that people doubt until they see it work? The thing a client pushed back on and then thanked you for.
- If the whole industry had to listen to you for thirty seconds, what would you tell them to stop doing?
- What are you sick of seeing everybody in your space do the exact same way?

Every time they name something for the left column, ask what they do instead and write the right column beside it. Push for volume. Do not stop at one; keep sweeping until they have at least four or five rows, and say out loud that you are collecting, not deciding.

Show them the finished table before moving on.

## Kill the fakes

Now go row by row and test each one, and be blunt about it:

- Would a competitor actually argue with this, or would everyone in the industry nod along? Nodding means it is a platitude, cut it or dig for the sharper version underneath.
- Does the right column come from something they have actually lived, or is it a line they heard somewhere? A take with no scar behind it is somebody else's opinion wearing their name.
- Who does believing this cost them? Name the customer they would lose. A take that costs nothing convinces nobody.
- If someone fully believed this, what would they go do next, and does that road lead to what the owner sells? A take that does not point at their offer is a hot take, not positioning.
- Would they still defend this in ten years? A stance they will outgrow next quarter is a prison, not a position.

Rows that survive are their candidate takes. Keep them all.

## The credibility bank

The first question anyone has when they find this owner is why they should listen to them at all. The answer is whatever they have actually done, so build the bank: the wins, the losses, the years, the specific clients, the lessons from what they got right and what they got wrong. Everyone in their space is repeating the same generic advice shaped by nobody's experience, and this is the part that cannot be lifted off somebody else's transcript.

Tie each candidate take to the experience that earned it. Any take with nothing in the bank behind it goes to the bottom of the list.

## Expert or student, and be honest about it

Before filing anything, settle which one they are, and ask it plainly: have you already done the thing your audience wants to do?

If yes, they are the expert. The bank above is their proof and the takes get written from the far side of the thing.

If not, they are the student, and that is a real position with its own power, not a lesser one. They are the proxy: spending the money, the time, and the effort to go get this thing, and bringing people along so those people can skip the mistakes. Their bank becomes an interest bank instead: what they are testing right now, what they are trying, what just failed and what it taught them. Takes get written from inside the journey, and they say so.

Say the warning out loud once, because it matters more than anything else in this session: a student writing as though they were an expert gets found out. It always surfaces, and the damage is bigger than everything gained while it worked. Their honest position is the one that compounds. Write which one they are into the file so every hire that reads it writes from the right chair.

## Pick the one you lead with

From the survivors, have them choose the one they will lead with starting now, and say plainly that this is a starting pick, not a marriage. Then build the north star sentence with them, in this shape:

I believe [their audience] who want [core desire] should [the contrarian belief] not [the common belief].

Rerun it out loud until they would say it on camera without flinching.

## Name the enemy

The lead take points at an enemy, and the enemy is always a belief or a bad way of doing things, never a person and never a named competitor. Aiming at a person makes them a call-out account instead of a brand.

Write the enemy as the thing standing between their buyer and what that buyer wants. Their ICP file already holds the zero state and the hero state; the enemy is what keeps the buyer stuck in the first one. Get that sentence written down, because it is what people rally around later.

## Write it into brand/contrarian-take.md

Replace the stub with these headings:

- The Lead Take: one or two sentences in their words, sharp enough to argue with.
- The Brand Statement: the I believe sentence.
- The Enemy: what stands between the buyer and what they want.
- Why The Common Way Fails: the mechanism, not just the claim.
- Expert Or Student: which chair this owner writes from, stated plainly.
- The Credibility Bank: what only this owner can say, and which take each piece backs. For a student, this is the interest bank: what they are testing right now and what it is teaching them.
- The Rebuttal: the strongest argument against the lead take and the answer to it, because hires get challenged in comments and this is what they stand on.
- Candidate Takes: every surviving row from the sweep, left column and right column, waiting to be tested.
- Where It Shows Up: content opens with it, sales conversations lean on it, the landing page headline breathes it. It gets repeated until the audience can quote it back, and if the owner feels sick of saying it, it is starting to work.
- How This Gets Decided: the note below.

Keep Raw Material From the Brain Dump underneath, unchanged. The ore stays with the metal.

## How this gets decided, and say this out loud

Tell the owner the part most people never hear: they do not actually pick their contrarian take, their audience does. The lead take and the candidates all go into content over the coming weeks, and the one that wins is the one that gets replies, DMs, comments quoting it back, and follow-up questions. That is the signal. When one of them clearly pulls ahead, it gets promoted to the lead and becomes the enemy everything is built around.

Write that instruction into the file so a future session knows to rerun this skill and reshuffle the order once there is real reaction to read.

## The ending, always

1. Read the lead take, the brand statement, and the enemy back, then watch their reaction. If they do not feel slightly exposed, it is probably not contrarian enough yet, and say so honestly.
2. If nothing survived today, do not force one. File the sweep under Raw Material From the Brain Dump, add the contrarian take to business.md's Still to answer list, and move on. A forced take is worse than a pending one.
3. Save the work to GitHub by running the github agent's save-work skill at departments/it-systems/dev-team/github-agent/skills/save-work/.

## Done when

brand/contrarian-take.md holds a lead take that a competitor would argue with, the brand statement, the enemy written as what stands between the buyer and what they want, the credibility bank tying takes to real experience, the surviving candidates from the sweep, the rebuttal, where it shows up, and the note on how the audience decides the winner. No stub text survives and the work is saved.
