# Contrarian Take

## The Lead Take

Stop selling AI. Sell how much money it saves or how much more it makes. If the pitch needs the word "AI" to sound impressive, it's not making anyone money.

## The Brand Statement

I believe if you're an established business owner, you should judge an AI system by how much money it saves or makes you, not by how "AI" the pitch sounds or how cool the demo looks.

## The Enemy

The AI-first sales pitch. People selling the technology, the hype, and the impressive-looking demo instead of a system proven to save or make real money, then can't fix it when it breaks because they never actually understood what they built. That's what keeps an established owner doing everything by hand and paying staff to grind through it: every AI pitch they've run into has been more show than substance, so they've stopped trusting any of it.

## Why The Common Way Fails

Two failure points, not one:

**On the product side:** systems built to look impressive (long agent chains, gamified dashboards, unnecessary complexity) have more points of failure than systems built to just work, and they're usually built by people who can't actually debug them when they break. The ICP owner (see brand/icp.md) only needs one bad output to lose trust completely and go back to doing it by hand himself, permanently. Flash doesn't survive that first failure. Substance does.

**On the pitch side:** leading with "AI" instead of the outcome sets the wrong expectation. It either triggers the skeptic ("flashy, no real value") or triggers the hype-chaser looking for a magic fix, and neither is the buyer that actually converts and stays. The buyer who converts wants a specific number: hours back, money back. Leading with AI buries that number under a category most owners are already burned out on hearing about.

## The Rebuttal

**Strongest argument against the lead take:** "AI" is literally what gets attention right now. Content that name-drops Claude, ChatGPT, Higgsfield, and rides the trend is what goes viral. If the rule is never lead with AI, how does anyone find this business or understand what makes it different from a generic efficiency consultant?

**The answer:** the rule only governs the sales moment, not the content moment. Organic content should lead with AI and ride the trend wave hard, it's genuinely what earns attention, and it's fine as long as the content underneath has real value. The rule kicks in once someone is actually deciding whether to pay: the cold email, the cold call, the ad, the price justification. At that point "AI" stops being a hook and starts being a distraction from the only thing that actually closes a deal, a specific number for money saved or made. AI can still be mentioned in the offer itself, it just never leads it.

## Candidate Takes

All six rows from the sweep, kept as candidates the audience will help re-rank:

| # | LEFT (what the industry does) | RIGHT (what's believed instead) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build AI systems to look impressive on social media (spaceship UIs, gamified dashboards) | If it needs to look impressive to justify its price, it isn't actually saving anyone money |
| 2 | Fabricate results or expertise (claim millions, claim experience you don't have) to sell the AI-agency dream | Market as ambitiously as you want, but never pretend to be further along than you actually are |
| 3 | Make the system look sophisticated (long chains, many tools) because complexity reads as expertise | The best system is the simplest one that does the job, complexity you can't maintain is a liability you're selling the client |
| 4 | Assemble no-code templates with no real understanding of what's happening underneath, so when it breaks, nobody can fix it | The tool doesn't matter, no-code or custom code, what matters is whether you actually understand and can maintain the system when it breaks |
| 5 | Start from "what cool AI thing can I build" and go looking for a business to bolt it onto | Start from the business's actual bottleneck, and only bring in AI if it's genuinely the cheapest way to solve it |
| 6 (lead) | Sell "AI" as the product itself, in the pitch | Lead with the outcome, money saved or made. AI is the vehicle, never the pitch, once someone's deciding whether to pay |

## Where It Shows Up

- **Organic content** (Threads, IG, any social hook): leads with AI, names the tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Higgsfield, etc.), rides the trend. This is the one place the lead take does not apply, attention here runs on the opposite rule.
- **Cold outreach, cold email, cold calls**: leads with the outcome, a specific money or time number, never with "AI" as the headline.
- **Sales calls and price justification**: same rule, doubled down, this is the moment the lead take exists for.
- **Landing page headline**: breathes the money outcome, not the AI feature.
- **The free audit / consultation offer**: framed as "see what this is costing you," not "see our AI."
