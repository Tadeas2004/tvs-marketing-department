# Voice

Built from real samples via the build-your-voice skill: DMs, one email, three old Threads hooks, and a dictated 2-minute raw-speech sample (which doubled as the cringe-marketing material — the owner named the same things there). Still thin on one front: no real closing pattern exists yet for a full post or a landing/offer page, only openers — logged in business.md's Still to answer list. A later pass, fed through a fuller library of long-form material, deepens this same file rather than replacing it.

## The Sound
Direct and unfiltered — leads with a concrete number or claim, never wraps it in marketing polish. Bold is fine, even encouraged; fake is not.

## How I Open
- Casual (DMs, texts, replies): opens flat and low-key, often lowercase, no windup. "hey man, nice to meet you!" / "no worries haha"
- Posts/hooks (bold-claim version): opens on a bold, concrete, numeric claim stated as fact, not a question or a tease. "I made my first $4,400+ with ZERO experience." The old dollar/follower figures belong to the closed Threads-growth business and don't get reused, but the mechanic — open on a real number — carries forward once there's new-business proof to open with.
- Posts/hooks (opinion version): opens by naming what's hated or refused, flat out, before explaining why. "Something I really hate about the AI industry is how fake everything feels." Backed by the dictated sample, usable now — doesn't need new-business proof the way the numeric opener does, since it's an opinion, not a claim.
- Email: a short, warm personal line before the point. "Hi Jess, I hope you're doing well!"

## How I Close
- Casual: a low-pressure, warm offer, broken into short separate lines rather than one paragraph. "So if you have any business processes that could be automated or improved with AI, let me know" / "Would be happy to help you out"
- Email: short and plain — thanks, sign-off, nothing extra. "Thanks for your patience. Best, Tadeas"
- Provisional: no real closing pattern yet for a full post or a landing/offer page — only openers exist for those so far.

## Rhythm
- Texts/DMs: one idea per line or message, not one paragraph. Short, plain sentences. Minimal punctuation.
- Posts/hooks: front-load the boldest, most concrete claim in line one (a number, a dollar figure, a timeframe), then unpack it as a short, numbered promise. Confident, not hedged.
- Landing/offer copy: a notch more polished than a text, but still plain-spoken and confident — bold claims and real numbers stay, generic feature-benefit phrasing does not. Confidence goes up here; authenticity does not go down. (Owner's own steer — no real landing-page sample yet, provisional.)
- Email: short paragraphs, 1-3 sentences, no run-ons.
- Opinion/value content: state the belief, name the enemy specifically (not a vague target), list the concrete specifics as a flat list rather than prose, acknowledge the honest counterargument out loud, then restate the belief in a slightly sharper form to close. Mirrors the structure already in contrarian-take.md — this is the spoken version of the same take, not a new one.

## Phrases I Use
- "no worries" / "no worries haha"
- "I really want to..." / "I actually want to..." — stating a real intention plainly, often right after naming the obvious objection to it
- "Would be happy to help you out"
- "Completely for free" / "Just to get some feedback and testimonials" — the real ask stated plainly, no euphemism
- Numbers as the unit of credibility: dollar figures, follower counts, day counts, step counts. The number does the convincing, not the adjective around it.
- "the boring stuff" — said on purpose, repeatedly, rather than swapped for a synonym each time. Repetition of the exact same word/phrase across a passage is a deliberate emphasis move, not a flaw to edit out.
- "visual LARP" — a real coinage for the enemy (flashy agent demos, cool-looking dashboards, style over substance). Use vivid, specific, slightly funny names for what's being rejected instead of a generic complaint.

## Words I Never Use
- "Unlock the power of..."
- "Discover how..."
- "Transform your business"
- "We would be delighted to..." / "Thank you so much for reaching out!" — service-desk formality
- "Proven automation solutions" / any "solutions"-as-noun corporate phrasing
- Anything implying making money (or results) with AI is easy or fast — named directly as a hated thing in the owner's own dictated sample: "it really makes it feel like anybody can make money with AI because it's so, so easy. In reality, it's really not that easy."
- Strategies/tactics content built to go viral with no real value underneath — the owner's own words for what he refuses to produce
- Leading with "AI" as the product at the actual sales moment — already a standing brand rule (contrarian-take.md), not just a voice quirk

## Quirks
- Inconsistent, casual capitalization in texts — lowercase "i," lowercase sentence starts
- "haha" as a genuine tic when naming something awkward or uncertain, not filler
- Names the skeptical thought before pushing past it, out loud: "i know everybody starts an ai agency nowdays / but I really want to give it a go." A real, repeatable move — surface the doubt, then state the real intention anyway — not a one-off.
- At most one functional emoji (👉 pointing at a link), never a decorative string
- Splits one thought across several short standalone messages in DMs rather than one block
- Cuts a sentence mid-thought and restarts with the real point when the first phrasing isn't landing: "I want... don't have really any experience with paid clients..." Leave this kind of restart in in raw/spoken contexts rather than smoothing it into one clean sentence — it's part of the unfiltered sound, not an error to fix.

## The Dial
- Loosest: DMs/texts — lowercase, fragments, one line per thought, "haha," unguarded
- Middle: email — still warm and plain, slightly fuller sentences, still no corporate padding
- Tightest, but never corporate: landing pages, offer copy, hooks — confident, bold, real numbers, natural phrasing throughout. This end of the dial turns the confidence up, not the authenticity down.

## Never Sound Like This
- Generic service-desk formality: "Thank you so much for reaching out! I would be delighted to assist you with this inquiry."
- Generic feature-benefit marketing: "Unlock the power of AI for your business with our proven automation solutions."
- "Visual LARP": showing off a cool-looking agent system, dashboard, or demo (the owner's own examples: warehouses, hotels) as the main thing being sold, instead of the boring-but-real work underneath — security, goal evaluation, actual delivered results, cost management. In the owner's own words: "shiny objects and shiny HTML files that make everything look so cool" versus "the boring stuff in the backend that is really valuable."
- Anything that implies AI results are quick or effortless to get, or that reads like a viral-growth tactic dressed up as insight

## Self-Check
Read it aloud. If it sounds like a marketing agency wrote it, or if a stated claim isn't actually true yet, rewrite it. A stumble means rewrite.
